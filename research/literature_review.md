# Literature Review: Automatic Solar Filament Instance Segmentation in Full-Disk H-Alpha Observations

## 1. Domain Context: Solar Filaments and Ground-Based H-Alpha Synoptic Networks
Solar filaments (termed *prominences* when observed projecting above the solar limb) are dense, cool ($T \approx 10^4\,\text{K}$), magnetically suspended plasma structures embedded within the hot ($T \approx 10^6\,\text{K}$), tenuous solar corona along magnetic polarity inversion lines (PILs) [Parenti, 2014]. Because destabilization of sheared or twisted filament flux ropes is a primary progenitor of coronal mass ejections (CMEs) and geomagnetic storms, automated, high-precision tracking of individual filament instances across full-disk Hydrogen-alpha ($\text{H-}\alpha$, $6562.8\,\text{\AA}$) synoptic observations is a cornerstone of operational space weather forecasting.

The Global Oscillation Network Group (GONG) operates six geographically distributed ground-based observatories—Big Bear (`Bb`), Cerro Tololo (`Ct`), Learmonth (`Le`), Mauna Loa (`Ml`), Teide (`Td`), and Udaipur (`Ud`)—capturing full-disk $2048 \times 2048$ $\text{H-}\alpha$ imagery continuously [Harvey et al., 1996]. Automated segmentation across this ground-based network faces four fundamental physical and observational challenges:
1. **Extreme Scale Disparity and Thin Curvilinear Morphology**: Quiescent and active-region filaments frequently exhibit spines only $2\text{--}6\,\text{px}$ wide at native $2048 \times 2048$ resolution while extending hundreds of pixels in length with delicate lateral barbs.
2. **Site-Dependent Atmospheric Seeing and Center-to-Limb Darkening**: Radial limb darkening, terrestrial atmospheric turbulence, and instrument-specific exposure curves create severe non-stationary background intensity gradients across the six GONG stations.
3. **Fragmentation vs. Instance Adjacency**: Along a single magnetic polarity inversion line, plasma condensations often appear partially fragmented or tightly clustered, creating ambiguity between disconnected segments of one physical filament and distinct neighboring filaments.
4. **Inherent Inter-Observer Plurality**: In the Manually Annotated GONG Filaments in H-Alpha Observations (MAGFiLO) benchmark [Ahmadzadeh et al., 2024], multiple solar physics annotators independently traced the same solar disk. Our empirical audit of the 707 competition training frames ($1{,}154$ annotator records, $8{,}199$ filament polygons) reveals that scoring one human annotator against another yields a Panoptic Quality of only $\text{PQ} = 0.4494$ ($\text{SQ} = 0.6766$, $\text{RQ} = 0.6642$).

## 2. Evolution of Solar Filament Segmentation Methods

### 2.1 Classical Thresholding, Morphological, and Curvilinear Filtering
Early automated filament catalogs relied on global/adaptive intensity thresholding coupled with region growing, active contours, or morphological closing after radial limb-darkening correction. While effective for high-contrast quiescent filaments, intensity-only methods suffer high false-positive rates around sunspot umbrae/penumbrae and chromospheric fibrils. Multi-scale Hessian eigenvalue filters—most notably the Sato curvilinear ridge filter [Sato et al., 1998]—and morphological black top-hat operators selectively amplify dark tubular structures of prescribed characteristic radii while suppressing isotropic sunspots and broad intensity gradients. In our architecture, rather than using classical operators as brittle standalone classifiers, we embed them as domain-specific input channels (`black top-hat`, `median background subtraction`, and `multi-scale Sato ridge response`) normalized by solar-disk Median Absolute Deviation (MAD).

### 2.2 Full-Frame Semantic Segmentation and Its Breakdown Under Panoptic Quality
With deep convolutional networks, U-Net and nested U-Net++ architectures [Zhou et al., 2018] became standard for solar feature extraction. However, applying full-frame semantic segmentation followed by connected-component labeling fails under the Panoptic Quality ($\text{PQ}$) metric [Kirillov et al., 2019] for two reasons:
- **Spatial Downsampling Loss**: Downsampling a $2048 \times 2048$ disk to $512 \times 512$ or $1024 \times 1024$ reduces $3\,\text{px}$ filament spines to sub-pixel width ($\le 1.5\,\text{px}$), causing severe false negatives ($\text{RQ} = 0.4641$, $\text{PQ} = 0.2840$ in our E01 baseline).
- **Instance Merging and Splitting Penalty**: Under $\text{PQ} = \text{SQ} \times \text{RQ}$, a predicted instance is credited as a True Positive ($\text{TP}$) if and only if its Intersection-over-Union ($\text{IoU}$) with a ground-truth instance strictly exceeds $0.50$. Merging two collinear filaments into one component converts one $\text{TP}$ into a False Positive ($\text{FP}$) and two False Negatives ($\text{FN}$).

### 2.3 Single-Stage vs. Decoupled Two-Stage Instance Segmentation
Modern single-stage instance segmenters such as YOLO11-seg [Jocher and Qiu, 2024] attach a prototype mask head operating at stride $4$ ($320 \times 320$ for a $1280 \times 1280$ input). While fast and effective at bounding-box localization ($\text{PQ} = 0.4050$, E02), low-resolution prototype combinations blur fine filament barbs and struggle to separate intertwined instances. Conversely, decoupling **full-frame instance detection** (via anchor-free YOLO11 [Jocher and Qiu, 2024] and two-stage Faster R-CNN with RoIAlign [Ren et al., 2015]) from **native-resolution instance-conditioned crop segmentation** ($512 \times 512$ zoom-in on each padded bounding box using U-Net++ [Zhou et al., 2018] with EfficientNet-B4 [Tan and Le, 2019], ResNet-34 [He et al., 2016], and SAM 2.1 Hiera-B+ [Ravi et al., 2024]) preserves above-native spatial resolution (median box side $\approx 130\,\text{px}$ magnified to $512\,\text{px}$) and elevates single-model validation $\text{PQ}$ from $0.4050$ to $0.4716$ ($+0.0666\,\text{PQ}$).

### 2.4 Semi-Supervised Noisy Student Self-Training and Multi-Family Mask Fusion
Because multi-annotator solar datasets are costly to curate while unlabeled synoptic GONG archives contain millions of frames, semi-supervised self-training with pseudo-labels [Lee, 2013; Xie et al., 2020] provides a principled mechanism to expand detector training diversity across solar cycle phases and atmospheric conditions. Finally, extending Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) [Solovyev et al., 2021] from bounding boxes to continuous instance probability maps and cross-family confidence calibration directly optimizes the non-differentiable $\text{IoU} > 0.50$ step function of Panoptic Quality.
